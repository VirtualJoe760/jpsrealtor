# The Complete Guide to 55+ Communities in the Coachella Valley

## Why the Coachella Valley for 55+ living

The Coachella Valley has more than a dozen age-restricted communities, from 4,900-home Sun City Palm Desert to boutique enclaves of a few hundred villas. Entry prices run from the low $300,000s for a villa to well over $1 million for a golf-front home, and HOA dues range from under $300 to about $680 a month.

This guide profiles the valley's best-known 55+ communities, compares them side by side, and explains the costs, healthcare access and seasonal realities that matter before you buy. Prices and HOA figures come from recent MLS listings and should be confirmed for any specific home.

A few corrections to common descriptions are worth knowing up front:

- **Sun City Shadow Hills** is in Indio (north of I-10), not La Quinta.

- **Villa Portofino** (often misspelled "Via Portafino") is in Palm Desert near Monterey Avenue and I-10, not the Indio–Bermuda Dunes corridor.

- **Trilogy** is two separate communities: Trilogy at La Quinta (golf) and Trilogy at The Polo Club in Indio. Neither is in Palm Desert.

- California property taxes are moderate rather than low: roughly 1% of purchase price plus local assessments, though Prop 13 caps annual increases.

## At a glance

Sun City Shadow Hills and Sun City Palm Desert offer the most home for the money; Trilogy at La Quinta and Trilogy at The Polo Club sit at the top of the market. Medians below are recent closed sales from the local MLS (fall 2026) unless marked as list prices.

HOA dues are what recent listings report and change yearly; confirm on the HOA disclosure for any home. Villa Portofino's higher dues include water, gas, insurance and building maintenance, which most single-family HOAs do not.

## Community profiles

Each profile links to its subdivision page with current listings, HOA details and sold history.

### [Sun City Palm Desert](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/palm-desert/sun-city)

The valley's largest 55+ community: about 4,950 single-level homes on roughly 1,600 acres off Varner Road, built by Del Webb from 1992 to 2003. It opened as Sun City Palm Springs and was renamed when residents joined Palm Desert's postal area.

**Community website:** [scpdca.com](https://www.scpdca.com/)

- **Homes:** 45 floor plans, mostly 2 bedrooms and 1,550–2,130 sq ft, all with 2- or 3-car garages. Villas start near $320,000.

- **Amenities:** three clubhouses, two Billy Casper–designed 18-hole courses at Mountain Vista Golf Club (pay-as-you-play, open to the public), indoor and outdoor pools, two restaurants, a library, post office and more than 80 clubs.

- **HOA:** about $440–490 a month, including cable, internet and exterior pest control.

- **Best for:** buyers who want the biggest social calendar and the most choice at a mid-range price.

### [Sun City Shadow Hills](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/indio/sun-city-shadow-hills)

Del Webb's second valley Sun City: about 3,400 homes in north Indio, guard-gated, built mostly 2004–2013. It is the best value among the large amenity communities, with a median sale around $455,000.

**Community website:** [scshca.com](https://www.scshca.com/)

- **Homes:** popular plans include the Canterra, Serrente and Sorrano, mostly 1,480–2,150 sq ft.

- **Amenities:** two golf courses (fees apply), including a Par 3 North Course ranked #4 among U.S. public short courses for 2026; two clubhouses, three pools (one indoor), pickleball, tennis, a dog park and 50+ clubs.

- **HOA:** about $400 a month, including internet and 24-hour guarded gates.

- **Best for:** golfers and value-focused buyers who want the full Sun City lifestyle for less.

### [Del Webb Rancho Mirage](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/rancho-mirage/del-webb-rm)

The newest resale option: about 1,000 homes on 380 gated acres near Dinah Shore and Bob Hope Drive, opened in 2018 and sold out in 2024. It is the most central location here, minutes from Eisenhower Health and The River.

- **Homes:** 10 plans (Getaway, Sanctuary, Refuge and others) from about 1,440 to 2,730 sq ft; many have owned solar. Recent listings run roughly $600,000 to $1.1 million.

- **Amenities:** The Outlook clubhouse with indoor and outdoor pools, fitness center, golf simulator, pickleball, tennis, bocce and six miles of walking trails. No golf course on site.

- **HOA:** $530 a month, now including cable and internet.

- **Best for:** buyers who want a near-new home and a central location over on-site golf.

### [Villa Portofino](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/palm-desert/villa-portofino)

A boutique, Italian-style community of about 460 villas and condos at Country Club Drive and Monterey Avenue in north Palm Desert. It is the most "lock and leave" option in the guide.

**Community website:** [villaportofinoliving.com](https://www.villaportofinoliving.com/)

- **Homes:** mostly 1- and 2-bedroom condos and villas, about 1,280–1,930 sq ft, built 2005 onward. Recent sales median is about $380,000.

- **Amenities:** a 30,000 sq ft clubhouse with a bistro, movie theater, yoga studio, fitness center, library, resort pool and a nine-hole putting course with water features. Walking distance to shopping; minutes to Eisenhower Health and El Paseo.

- **HOA:** about $820–1,000 a month, but it covers water, gas, building insurance, roofs and grounds.

- **Best for:** part-time residents and downsizers who want no exterior upkeep.

### [Trilogy at La Quinta](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/la-quinta/trilogy)

Shea Homes' guard-gated golf community of about 1,240 homes south of Highway 111, built 2003–2008 against the Santa Rosa and Coral mountains.

**Community website:** [mytrilogylife.com/laquinta](https://mytrilogylife.com/laquinta)

- **Homes:** plans such as the Oasis, Monterey and Cassius, typically 1,700–2,200 sq ft. Median sale about $575,000, with golf-front homes over $1 million.

- **Amenities:** an 18-hole championship course that once hosted the PGA Tour's Skins Game, two restaurants, two large pools, fitness center, tennis, pickleball and bocce.

- **HOA:** about $620 a month.

- **Best for:** serious golfers who want mountain views and a resort feel.

### [Trilogy at The Polo Club](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/indio/trilogy-polo-club)

About 1,000 homes near the Empire Polo grounds in south Indio, mixing 55+ and all-ages neighborhoods. It has the highest median price in this guide, at about $680,000.

**Community website:** [mytrilogylife.com/polo](https://www.mytrilogylife.com/polo)

- **Homes:** newer, contemporary plans (many built 2017–2023), mostly 1,600–2,300 sq ft; small "Resort Collection" homes start near 1,300 sq ft.

- **Amenities:** the private 25,000+ sq ft Polo Club with June Hill's Table restaurant, bar, fitness center, resort and lap pools, tennis, pickleball, indoor golf simulator and a lake stage.

- **HOA:** about $280 a month; club membership is separate. One listing quoted a $20,000 buy-in, or $3,000 when a membership transfers with the home.

- **Best for:** buyers who want a modern home and are fine paying for the club.

### [Four Seasons at Terra Lago](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/indio/four-seasons-at-terra-lago)

K. Hovnanian's guard-gated community of about 680 homes off Golf Center Parkway in north Indio, built 2014–2021. It is the newest home for the lowest HOA in the guide.

**Community website:** [fourseasonsatterralago.com](https://fourseasonsatterralago.com/)

- **Homes:** mostly 1,700–2,500 sq ft, single-story; recent listings around $400,000–550,000.

- **Amenities:** The Lodge, a 22,000 sq ft clubhouse with indoor and outdoor pools, fitness center, salon, ballroom, bistro, tennis, pickleball and 50+ clubs. The public Golf Club at Terra Lago is next door.

- **HOA:** about $275–300 a month.

- **Best for:** buyers who want a newer home with low monthly costs.

### [Heritage Palms](https://jpsrealtor.com/neighborhoods/indio/heritage-palms-country-club)

A guard-gated golf community of about 1,000 homes on 379 acres in south Indio, built 1998–2004.

**Community website:** [heritagepalmsgolfclub.com](https://www.heritagepalmsgolfclub.com/)

- **Homes:** 2- and 3-bedroom homes, about 1,400–2,950 sq ft; recent listings around $525,000–650,000.

- **Amenities:** an Arthur Hills–designed 18-hole course with no initiation fee, a 20,000 sq ft clubhouse, indoor and outdoor pools, fitness center, pickleball and tennis. Shopping at Heritage Court is within golf-cart distance.

- **HOA:** about $600–680 a month, including cable and internet. Buyers pay about $4,800 in one-time reserve fees at closing.

- **Best for:** golfers who want course access without club dues.

## What it really costs

The sticker price is only part of the budget. Plan for five recurring costs beyond the mortgage:

- **HOA dues.** From about $280 (Trilogy at The Polo Club, before club fees) to about $1,000 (Villa Portofino, which bundles utilities and insurance). Check what each includes; cable and internet are now common.

- **Property tax.** In California, the base rate is 1% of your purchase price, plus local voter-approved assessments; most valley addresses land around 1.1–1.3% in year one. Prop 13 then limits increases on assessed value to 2% a year.

- **Special assessments (Mello-Roos / CFD).** Newer Indio and Rancho Mirage communities may carry these, adding hundreds to thousands per year. Ask for the tax bill before you make an offer.

- **Golf and club fees.** Sun City and Sun City Shadow Hills courses are pay-as-you-play. Heritage Palms has no initiation fee. Trilogy at The Polo Club's private club is a separate membership.

- **Utilities.** Summer cooling is the big one. Most of Indio, La Quinta and much of Palm Desert are served by Imperial Irrigation District (IID), generally cheaper than Southern California Edison, which serves Rancho Mirage. Owned solar, common at Del Webb Rancho Mirage, changes the math.

Also budget for one-time transfer and reserve fees at closing; Heritage Palms, for example, collects about $4,800.

If you are 55 or older and selling a California home, **Prop 19** lets you carry your current tax base to a new home anywhere in the state, up to three times. That can cut the property tax on a desert purchase dramatically. Confirm eligibility with the Riverside County Assessor.

**Questions to ask before you buy:**

- What exactly do HOA dues cover, and when did they last increase?

- How healthy is the reserve fund? Any special assessments planned?

- Is there a Mello-Roos or CFD assessment on the tax bill?

- Are golf, the club or the fitness center included, pay-as-you-play or separate membership?

- What are the rental rules? Several communities require 30-day minimums.

- What are the guest rules for visitors under 55?

- Which utility serves the home, IID or Edison? Is there owned or leased solar?

- How far is the home from the clubhouse, and is a golf cart practical?

Visit during the season (November–April) when clubs are active, and again in summer to see what the community is like with fewer residents.

## Living in the valley

Winters are the reward: sunny, dry days in the 70s from November through April, when most clubs and events run at full speed. Summers are the price: highs regularly pass 105°F from June through September, and many residents travel or keep a second home elsewhere.

- **Events:** Palm Springs International Film Festival (January), BNP Paribas Open tennis in Indian Wells (March), and the Coachella and Stagecoach festivals in Indio (April). Indio-area communities see heavy traffic on festival weekends.

- **Culture and shopping:** El Paseo in Palm Desert, the McCallum Theatre, and The River in Rancho Mirage.

- **Getting around:** Palm Springs International Airport is roughly 15–30 minutes from these communities. Los Angeles and San Diego are about two to three hours by car.

- **Seasonal rentals:** many communities allow 30-day-plus rentals, which can offset costs for part-time owners. Rules vary, so confirm with the HOA.
